2.2 Labelling the points

Every point carries two labels: a segment (ground, low, building, tree, shrub, vehicle, wall, street furniture, podium tree, porous facade, unsure) and the object it belongs to: one building part, one tree, one car. The nine USGS tiles are cut into 500 m chunks, each with a 50 m margin for the points around it, and labelled one at a time in Houdini. (site-model/houdini/tools/split_chunks.py)

Buildings and trees are told apart by a random forest trained on the shape of the points around each return. Its training labels come only from where a point is (inside a LARIAC building outline, or by a tree far from any outline), never from the shapes it learns, so it can judge the points in between; it is tested on 50 m blocks it did not see. (site-model/houdini/tools/train_seg_forest.py)

Building points are grouped by the LARIAC 2020 outlines (BLD_ objects) or, where no outline exists, as structures found in the LiDAR alone (PC_). Trees are first split at every local top and then joined again where the canopy hardly dips between two tops; leftover points join the building or tree they touch. Cars are checked last: low objects are scored against the cars already found, and every candidate is looked at before it is relabelled. (site-model/houdini/tools/refine_objects.py, car_sweep.py)

2.4 Estimated facades

Airborne LiDAR sees roofs well and walls badly: glass curtain walls reflect the laser away and the near-vertical view grazes the walls. Points are added on the walls of each building's stepped volume, only where no measured return is near, mostly on the storey lines read from the building's own wall returns, and not in the lowest metres above the ground (arcades, entrances and plazas stay open). They thin with distance like the measured cloud. They are estimates, not measurements; the viewer draws them with the measured points of their building. (site-model/houdini/tools/synth_facades.py)

2.5 Terrain

The ground is Houdini's ground model of the LiDAR: every point keeps its height above it. For the analysis layers it is written as a 2 m grid over 2.5 by 2.5 km around Y-1 (1,250 by 1,250 cells), each cell the mean ground height of the returns in it, under buildings too; 0.2% of the cells had no returns and take the nearest measured cell. Heights are NAVD88: the street at Y-1 is about 90 m. In the viewer the ground is the Terrain points, thinned in irregular 60 m patches away from the site. (site-model/houdini/tools/export_handoff.py → handoff/terrain.npy)

The ground here is not the original hill: the CRA's clearance in the 1960s took some 30 feet off the top of Bunker Hill (bh-008).

4Research

4.1 The entries

What is known about Parcel Y-1 is kept as entries, each with its sources, a status (verified, unverified, open question, our analysis) and the date it was last checked. They are read in Research, List; the rules for checking them are in RESEARCH.md: every quoted excerpt is fetched again and compared with its source, and an entry the sources do not support is marked down, not deleted.

4.2 The network

Research, Network draws the same entries in 3D, height for date. Its links come only from the entries: one entry naming another, an entry naming a person or organisation (from a list of names and their other forms, every match read), a Media comment and its subject (our grouping), and two entries citing the same source. Each link keeps the sentence or source that shows it. (docs/connections_build.py)
