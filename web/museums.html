LA Museum Economy

Angel’s Knoll / Y-1 · MOCA and the Broad are the site’s neighbours on Grand Avenue

None of these museums lives on ticket sales. MoMA is the most self-funded and even there admissions are about 15% of operating revenue, while its shops and publishing bring in twice as much. The LA museums run on donors, plus county money at LACMA. The Petersen is the exception: admissions are its single largest stream.

Method, basis and caveats

Not one fiscal year. These six do not share one. MoMA, LACMA, FAMSF and MOCA report years ending 30 June 2025; the Broad and the Petersen file on the calendar year and their latest complete year is 2024. The two windows overlap from July to December 2024. Each pie is labelled with its own period.

Positive slices only. Pies show the streams that brought money in. Negative lines are left out: LACMA −$2.0M interest-rate swap, MOCA −$1.22M net fundraising events, the Broad −$1.88M on asset sales, the Petersen −$0.40M events and −$0.15M asset sales. Reported totals are therefore lower than the sum of the slices, and both numbers are in the table.

Where the numbers come from. MoMA, LACMA and FAMSF are audited statements and the FAMSF annual report. MOCA, the Broad and the Petersen are read line by line from IRS filings — Form 990 Part VIII, and Form 990-PF Parts I and XV-A for the Broad.

  • The Broad files as a private operating foundation, so its shape is unusual. Its largest line is $11.63M of GAAP discount on pledges — an accounting entry, not cash from operating the museum. It sits inside “Other” here. Its ticketed exhibitions ($1.02M) and shop ($1.55M gross profit) are reported, despite the 990-PF not itemising them the way a 990 does.
  • MOCA has had free general admission since January 2020; the $0.83M of admissions is ticketed special exhibitions and programmes. Membership ($0.91M) and government grants ($0.15M) are broken out on line 1 of its 990.
  • LACMA’s “Other” is large and mostly unexplained. $40.9M of it sits in “all other program service revenue” with no itemisation on the 990 or in Schedule O. Its program service revenue rose from $45.3M to $87.2M year over year, which is consistent with a one-off insurance settlement, but the filing does not say so.
  • MoMA’s shops line is retail and publishing, not dining. Gross inventory sales were $83.3M; the restaurants line is $1.37M, filed under the food service contractors business code, and Schedule R lists a retail entity but no restaurant entity.
  • Auxiliary folds in parking where a museum reports it — $2.90M at the Petersen, $2.41M at the Broad.

Colours are the data-viz reference categorical palette. The wedge order around each pie is not arbitrary: it was chosen by running every circular arrangement through the colour-vision validator and keeping one that clears the separation floors on all seven seams in both light and dark mode (worst CVD ΔE 9.2, worst normal-vision ΔE 19.3). Three of the light-mode hues sit under 3:1 contrast, so the table above carries the same figures.
